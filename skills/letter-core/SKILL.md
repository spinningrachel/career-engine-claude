# Letter Core — the letter-writer's complete working doctrine

This is the ONLY doctrine file the letter-writer loads. Everything else about letters is enforced at the gatekeeper — when a violation comes back, it arrives with the rule quoted and a fix direction; fix that, touch nothing else. Write plainly from her material and you will pass.

---

## 1. The job: assemble her material, don't compose

**The rule, in the user's own words (2026-07-24): "open the letter with context using the outline, templates, verbatim banks etc. to write that sentence and the rest of the letter, and adding additional content insofar as may prove to be necessary AFTER an exhaustive search of all available insights inside the /career-data skill."** That is the whole method — the outline gives the structure, her templates and verbatim banks give the sentences, and composing anything new is the last resort, not a parallel track.

The letter is built FROM the user's existing words, aimed at this role. Her material: the role-matched **Motivation Bank** rows, **delivered-letter** phrasings and openers (read at the Voice Gate), **`03-framework.md`** lines, and **Why I Want This Role** when present. Reusing her sentences verbatim is not a shortcut — it is the method. Her proven phrasing lands better than anything composed fresh, and she recognizes it as hers. **Fresh content is permitted only AFTER an exhaustive search of ALL of career-data comes up short for that specific need** — not just the Bank and delivered letters, but `03-framework.md` and its sub-files, the role-facts files, approved summaries and bullets, testimonials, and portfolio. "I didn't find it in the first two files I opened" is not exhaustion. When you do compose, it exists to connect her pieces or fill a genuine gap the whole of career-data couldn't — and keep it plain.

**Two kinds of her material, handled differently:**
- **Finished prose** (a Bank row written as sentences, a delivered-letter line): carry it intact. Do not paraphrase, polish, or "improve" it.
- **Note-form shorthand** (a telegraphic WIWTR jot — "Doing good. Win win."): that is *meaning*, not prose. Develop it into a sentence in her voice, keeping her exact words inside a grammatical frame. Never paste it raw; never erase it into polished-professional language either. Both directions lose her.

**Her emotion is reusable material.** Enthusiasm, reactions, and want-statements she has already expressed — in a delivered letter, the Bank, or framework — may be lifted or adapted wherever they are genuinely true for THIS role. Never invent emotion; never transplant a role-specific reaction to a role it doesn't fit.

## 2. Inputs and what each one does

- **The coach's outline** (`$PIPE/coach-outline.md`) — the letter's paragraph structure and order. Follow it. Each `Para` line is an argument plan: the **claim** the paragraph must establish, the documented work to **LEAD** with, and what stays **SUPPORT** (a clause at most). Open the paragraph on the LEAD and make the claim in plain terms — a passing mention is not making it. Legacy bare-subject lines mean topic only. Either way the outline carries no facts or phrasing; her material supplies what each paragraph says.
- **`Role emphasis`** — the content selector: what this role is for, so which Bank entries, WIWTR pieces, and proof points should carry the letter. Never a source of sentences or facts.
- **WIWTR** — her role-specific motivation, when present. All its distinct points appear in the letter, honestly labeled (Rule 9 below). It never sets paragraph order — it is raw material.
- **The final CV** — for the no-repetition rule only (Rule 6).
- **You never receive** `Role summary`, JD text, `Keywords`, `Culture`, `Landscape`, `Gap handling` content, or the recruiter review — and never read them from `$PIPE`. The letter is her material aimed at `Role emphasis`; company research never becomes letter text.

**One argument, connected.** Each paragraph picks up from the one before — a thread continued, a question answered. Before returning a draft, read the paragraphs' first sentences in sequence: one person making one case, in the outline's order.

## 3. The opener

**Open the letter with context, and write that sentence from her material — the outline, the templates, the verbatim banks (the user's rule, §1).** In practice: the coach's outline usually carries an `Opener: <...> (variant <n>)` line — one of her own template sentences, picked by the coach and user-reviewed in Notion. **Arriving placeholder-free, it is finished prose: paste it verbatim as the opening — zero rewriting, zero improvement, zero splicing (the gatekeeper diffs the letter text against it and hard-fails any silent rewrite; 2026-07-24, from a real run that composed over a ready reviewed sentence).** If blanks remain, fill only those from her material, frame verbatim. Done. If the outline has no such line (older rows), pick the fitting Block 1 variant from her `cover_letter_templates.md` (coach-selected template) yourself. Second choice: adapt a delivered-letter opener shape. Only when the whole bank genuinely can't be truthfully filled for this role: construct from the structures below. Whatever happened, write it down in `$PIPE/opener-derivation.txt` (`variant=<id>`, or `pattern=<n> | no-variant-because: <reason>`) — plain bookkeeping so the gatekeeper can see the opener came from her material.

**The opening paragraph is bounded by the template's own Block 1 — the filled Opener sentence plus at most the 1–2 further sentences the selected Block 1 shape itself calls for, each from her material (2026-07-24, per the user: "I'm worried I'll find myself deleting unnecessary badly written slop from the rest of every single opening para").** The outline never adds subjects to paragraph 1 — its paragraph subjects start at `Para 2:`, and a legacy outline's `Para 1:` line that re-describes the Opener is a duplicate spec: ignore it (it authorizes nothing beyond Block 1's own bounds). Never pad the opening with composed reaction, context-setting, or "why now" prose the Block 1 shape didn't ask for — if Block 1 is satisfied in two sentences, paragraph 1 is done in two sentences.

**Within two sentences the reader knows:** the role (named in sentence 1), why her, why now — all in words a stranger who knows only the company name can understand. **Context test:** no abstract stand-ins ("the challenge I've spent my career on") whose only definition is a riddle. Concrete referents: companies, disciplines, plain credentials.

**Plain beats clever, every time — and sentence 1 is the plainest sentence in the letter.** Sentence 1 does exactly two jobs: name the role, and state her want or reaction with a concrete plain-language credential. NO device in sentence 1 — no negation setup ("not a title I expected to want"), no general-truth aphorism or paradox ("...is only as trustworthy as the person explaining it"), no product-mechanism description, no echo-intensifier ("is exactly what I've already done"). A good story or observation from her material earns its place from sentence 2 onward, after context exists. No engineered punchline, setup-payoff twist, or metaphor-flavored flourish anywhere; given two renderings of the same content, choose the plainer.

**Professional fit leads; personal proof follows.** A personal fact opens the letter only when it IS the professional credential. Affinity and biography go mid-letter, after a proof anchor — placed, never explained.

**The 12 structures** (numbering matches writer-craft §9 exactly — the gatekeeper verifies `pattern=<n>` against that list; fill every bracket with her actual content):
1. **Direct parallel** — reaction to the overlap, then the causal credential ("Because I was just doing it, as [role] at [Company]...").
2. **Unfamiliar domain (transfer)** — lead with the transferable skill and breadth evidence, name what stays the same across domains. Never name the gap.
3. **Compliance/regulated buyer** — name the real buyer insight plainly, one thing built in response, land the application in one sentence.
4. **PLG/product-led role** — genuine documented passion statement → first exposure → range (success and failure) → what she knows now.
5. **Function-builder close** — founding mandate; three-sentence close: scope claim → emotional sentence → company landing ("I've built X from scratch. I loved it every time. I'd love to build it at [Company].").
6. **Multi-domain** — the same problem recurred across companies: horizontal competence claim grounded in breadth, or a rapid-fire one-clause-per-company list.
7. **Proof bullets** — 2-3 metric-backed outcomes as a short list after a confident setup line; only for a transferability/pivot letter or a multi-mandate role.
8. **Warm connection/referral** — name the person and context, move to proof.
9. **Anticipated question** — name the reader's likely question without apology; answer with proof.
10. **Problem-first** — HER documented observation (from WIWTR/Bank only), she is the subject of sentence 1.
11. **Value claim** — name what they need, pivot to her confident claim ("I'm a strategic builder..."), name how.
12. **Plain want** — "I want [Role] at [Company] because [a real, role-relevant reason from her material]," then 1-2 plain clauses. The reason must be in the same sentence and concrete; a hollow or biographical-non-sequitur because-clause disqualifies it.

### Annotated exemplar — the sentence mechanics to mirror

> *[Name] here.* — proper-noun subject, deliberate identity fragment, complete as a sentence.
> *I'll be honest. I nearly screamed when I saw this role.* — "I" as subject, short finite verb, two independent statements as two sentences.
> *Because I was just doing it over the last year as the [function] leader at [Company A], a direct competitor of [Company B] that [outcome].* — deliberate causal fragment; predicate complete in five words; all complexity trails AFTER the predicate as appositives.

What they share: a person up front, the verb early, detail trailing behind. Fragments are rare and each has a job — a fragment with no job is noise, not rhythm.

## 3b. Senior roles — one paragraph on what success looks like and how it's measured

*(2026-10-20, per the user: "for senior roles, letters should emphasize what success looks like - this doesn't have to be metrics/KPIs - but there should be a para that indicates to hiring managers that the user knows how they should be measured.")*

**Applies when the role is senior:** the role title carries Manager, Lead, Head, Director, VP / Vice President, Chief, GM, or Principal (or anything higher), **or** `Strategy = Strategic`. For these roles the letter carries one dedicated paragraph, between the proof and the close, showing the reader she knows what this seat is judged on.

- **Source: `Role emphasis` only** — its **Likely KPIs** bullets (and the Emphasis jobs they pair with) tell you what the role is measured on; her own results from the final CV / Motivation Bank / delivered letters supply the matching experience. No other role-analysis input exists for you — never reach for the JD, `Landscape`, or research.
- **Shape: 2-3 plain sentences.** Say what success in this seat looks like in terms a hiring manager would use (a motion that runs without her, adoption that sustains, a pipeline the sales team trusts, a launch cadence that holds) and tie it to how she has been measured before. It does **not** need numbers. When a number is hers and documented, use it exactly as documented; never invent a target, never promise a result.
- **Judged-on, not promised.** The paragraph shows she knows the measure ("I'd expect to be judged on…", "the real test of this seat is…") — it is not a 30-60-90 plan, not a pledge of outcomes, not a recital of the JD's responsibilities.
- **Counts inside the word ceiling.** Make room by trimming the weaker of two proof lines, never by dropping this paragraph or the close.
- **Not senior:** write no such paragraph; do not pad an IC letter with a KPI paragraph.

## 4. The letter's shape — five blocks (plus the senior-roles-only Success measure), each present and doing its job

Greeting → **Opening** → **Philosophy** (a belief/POV about the discipline in her voice — may be one sentence; never another proof point) → **Proof** (1-3 paragraphs, one job each, at least one named outcome) → **Objection-Preemption** (the reader's likely hesitation answered by demonstration — proof that speaks to it, with NO acknowledgment that a question exists; often lives inside a proof paragraph) → **Success measure** (senior roles only, §3b) → **Close** → Sign-off. The gatekeeper checks all five blocks (and the Success measure on senior roles) independently; a block that occupies the position without doing its job fails.

- **Greeting:** exactly "Hi to the [Company] team!" or "Hi to [Name]!" (stealth roles: "Hi to the team!" or "Hi to the [JD descriptor] team!"). Company name appears in paragraph 1 (stealth: the JD descriptor).
- **Body ceiling:** 320 words (`wc -w`, excluding greeting/sign-off); **250 when `Strategy = Strategic`**. No minimum — short and hers beats padded.
- **Paragraphs:** 3-4 lines max; the close is always its own paragraph — a direct, forward-looking, unhedged ask.
- **Sign-off:** "Looking forward to next steps," / her name (archive-consistent variation fine).

## 5-10. The absolutes

5. **No fabrication, ever.** Every claim traces to her documented background or her own words (WIWTR/Bank/framework/delivered letters — which are hers, not fabrication). No reviewer feedback ever authorizes invention. No claim documented for one role generalized to more; no prior-state claims about past employers beyond the documented record.
6. **Never repeat CV content.** Skip it, add something new, or enhance it with story/decision logic the CV can't carry — never restate. **Exception: the outline's `LEAD:` work.** If the plan requires evidence the CV also states (team leadership, owned scope), use it and add the decision, stakes, or reasoning the CV bullet lacks; do not swap it for a smaller, CV-absent artifact to dodge repetition (that traded leadership for a brochure in the IO River letter).
7. **No em dashes. No colons in body text.** Period, comma, or restructure.
8. **Never name a gap.** Nothing is "unfamiliar," "new to me," or "a gap" — check her guardrail table (`01-writing-rules.md` "do not flag as a gap" rows) FIRST; use its exact framing. A hesitation is answered by demonstration or by silence.
9. **Honest WIWTR labels.** `$PIPE/wiwtr-checklist.md`, three fields per point: her exact text, the letter's rendering (or "ABSENT"), and a true label — `verbatim` (her string is literally in the letter; it gets grepped), `adapted` (her words, connectors adjusted), `resolved-by-proof` (with why her phrasing couldn't be carried), or `set-aside` (with reason). "The topic is covered" is never `verbatim`.
10. **No company research in letter text.** Org size, funding, acquisitions, founding year, product descriptions recited back — none of it, regardless of grammatical subject, unless inside her own verbatim reaction. She is the subject of every paragraph, speaking from named experience.

**Style in one line:** she is the subject, verbs active and early, her rhythm (long comma-chained sentences punctuated by short ones that earn their place), the right word repeated rather than synonym-cycled, specifics over abstractions, one good example over three paragraphs of argument.

**Three words/moves that leak in from the pipeline — never write them:** "mandate" in letter prose (it's `Role emphasis`'s internal label — say what the job is in plain words); a fit-declaration ("this role was mine," "has my name on it," "perfect fit") unless verbatim hers; narrating that you read the posting ("I've read the posting twice," any version — a reaction is not a report of reading).

**Sparse notes are not a gap to fill.** Never expand a one-line WIWTR into an invented paragraph — check the Bank first; if nothing usable exists anywhere, write `[{{USER_FIRST_NAME}} TO FILL IN]` and move on. That is a correct outcome, not a failure.

## 11. Self-check (before returning any draft)

1. `$PIPE/opener-derivation.txt` written; the opener genuinely matches what it claims.
2. `$PIPE/wiwtr-checklist.md` written, three fields, labels true (grep your own `verbatim` claims).
3. Spine test: paragraph first-sentences read as one case, in the outline's order.
4. Zero-context test on sentences 1-2; role named in sentence 1; plainer rendering chosen.
5. `grep '—'` = zero; no colons in body.
6. `wc -w` body ≤ 320 (250 if Strategic).
7. Her words carried: the letter's best sentences are hers, not yours; shorthand developed, not pasted; nothing of hers erased into corporate polish.
8. No CV fact restated; every proof paragraph does one job; at least one named outcome.
9. No gap named; guardrail framings used where relevant.
10. Read it once as the hiring manager: a person you'd want to meet, or a form that passed checks?

Everything else — the full banned-word and phrase catalog, structural pattern bans, claim-verification detail — is enforced at the gatekeeper and comes back quoted with a fix if you hit it. Don't write toward the catalog; write plainly from her material.
