# Discipline Emphasis Signals

Read before writing any `Role emphasis` property (added 2026-07-23; two-part structure since 2026-07-24, bulleted JTBD since 2026-10-20). This file anchors the emphasis read: for each common discipline, the signals that reveal what a JD actually weights — and, critically, how the **same phrase means different work at different companies**, so the `Emphasis` line can translate an ambiguous term through the company's real operating model (research dimension 1), never through the phrase alone. **Name the motion in generic discipline vocabulary (PLG, B2D, ABM, enterprise sales-led, community-led)** — the writers, which read the user's own career-data, do the mapping to the specific user; `Role emphasis` itself never mentions the candidate.

**How to use it:** identify the role's discipline(s), scan that section's signals against the JD (frequency, placement, verb strength, space devoted), and let the "same term, different company" rows disambiguate what the emphasized theme actually means *here*. This is a reference to reason from, not a checklist to fill — most JDs light up 3-5 signals, and the ranked top 2-3 are what `**Emphasis:**` summarizes, in ≤100 words for the whole property.

---

## Marketing (demand gen, growth, PMM, content, brand, comms)

Top signals to weigh:
1. **Pipeline/revenue language density** — "pipeline," "SQLs," "revenue marketing" repeated = sales-led motion; near-absent = brand, product-led, or community motion.
2. **The company's actual GTM decides what "demand gen" means** — PLG company: demand gen = organic growth, activation, in-product lifecycle. Early-stage with no PLG surface and little pipeline talk: likely enterprise/ABM. Ad-supported consumer: audience growth and retention.
3. **Channel specificity** — named channels (SEO, paid, events, outbound, community) vs. generic "multi-channel" tells you whether they know what works yet (Builder vs. Scaler context).
4. **Content's position** — content as pipeline engine (owns funnel metrics) vs. content as support function (enablement, brand voice).
5. **Positioning/messaging ownership** — "own positioning," "narrative," "category" = strategic PMM altitude; "produce collateral," "support launches" = execution altitude.
6. **Sales-proximity verbs** — "partner with sales," "enablement," "ABM" = enterprise buyer; "self-serve," "conversion," "activation" = product-led buyer.
7. **Metrics named** — MQL/SQL/pipeline (sales-led) vs. activation/retention/PQL (PLG) vs. reach/engagement/community (brand/consumer). Absence of metrics = expectations undefined (also a Signals flag).
8. **Team shape** — first marketer / solo = full-stack breadth weighting; joining a function = the JD's named specialty is the real job.
9. **Technical-audience markers** — "developers," "technical buyers," "docs" = technical credibility and product depth outweigh classic campaign skills.
10. **Localization/regional language** — market entry or regional ownership = market-development capabilities weigh more than the title suggests.

## Sales / Business Development

1. Named quota or revenue figure vs. none (maturity of the motion).
2. "Land new logos" vs. "expand accounts" — hunter vs. farmer weighting.
3. Deal-size/cycle markers — "enterprise," "6-figure ACV," "procurement" vs. "high-velocity," "transactional."
4. Named verticals or territories = domain fluency weighting.
5. "Build the playbook" / "first AE" = founder-sales context, process creation over execution.
6. Partner/channel language = indirect motion capabilities.
7. Sales-engineering proximity ("technical demos," "POCs") = technical depth weighting.
8. Tooling named (Salesforce, outbound stacks) = ops discipline expected.

## Product Management

1. Verb altitude — "define strategy/vision" vs. "write specs, groom backlog": strategy vs. execution PM.
2. "0→1" / "new product line" vs. "optimize," "iterate" — Builder vs. Scaler mandate.
3. Technical-depth markers — "APIs," "platform," "infrastructure," "work with architects" = technical PM weighting.
4. Data language — "experimentation," "A/B," "metrics-driven" = growth-PM weighting.
5. Customer-contact verbs — "discovery," "user research" (weighted) vs. token mention.
6. Stakeholder surface — "exec alignment," "cross-org" = internal-influence weighting over craft.
7. Domain nouns repeated (payments, security, ML) = domain depth is a real screen, not flavor.

## Engineering

1. Stack specificity and repetition — the named technologies that appear 3+ times are the screen; single mentions are decoration.
2. "Design/architect" vs. "implement/ship" — system-design altitude vs. delivery speed.
3. Scale markers — traffic/data volumes, "distributed," "high availability" = scale experience weighting.
4. "Greenfield" vs. "legacy," "migration," "refactor" — build vs. fix mandate.
5. Ownership breadth — "end to end," "full stack," "you build it you run it" = generalist weighting; deep single-layer language = specialist.
6. Team-shape signals — "mentor," "raise the bar," "tech lead" = leadership weighting without the title.
7. Product-proximity language — "work directly with customers/product" = product-engineer weighting.

## Data (analytics, DS, ML)

1. Decision-language — "inform strategy," "partner with leadership" = analytics-translator weighting; "build models/pipelines" = engineering weighting.
2. Stack named (dbt, warehouse, Python/R, ML frameworks) and which appears most.
3. "Production" / "deploy" / "MLOps" = engineering rigor over analysis.
4. Experimentation language = product-analytics weighting.
5. Governance/quality language = infrastructure maturity mandate.

## Customer Success / Support / Ops

1. Revenue verbs ("renewal," "expansion," "NRR") = commercial CS vs. adoption/health CS.
2. Segment markers — enterprise named-accounts vs. scaled/pooled digital CS.
3. "Build the function" vs. "manage the book" — Builder vs. operator.
4. Technical-product depth ("implementation," "integrations," "APIs") = technical CS weighting.
5. Process/tooling language = ops-maturity mandate.

## HR / People

1. "Scale the team" hiring language vs. "develop, retain" — recruiting-weighted vs. development-weighted.
2. "First People hire" / "build programs" = generalist Builder.
3. Compliance/geography markers = operational depth weighting.
4. "Partner with leadership," "org design" = strategic HRBP altitude.

## Finance / Ops / BizOps

1. "Model," "forecast," "board materials" = FP&A altitude; "close," "controls," "audit" = accounting altitude.
2. Fundraising proximity = strategic-finance weighting.
3. "Ambiguous problems," "special projects" = BizOps generalist mandate.
4. Systems named (ERP, billing) = operational implementation weighting.

## Design / UX

1. "Own end-to-end" vs. "execute within a system" — altitude signal.
2. Research language weighted vs. token.
3. "Design system" ownership = infrastructure mandate.
4. Brand-vs-product balance — which surface the JD spends more lines on.

## Cross-discipline reading rules

- **Placement beats presence.** A theme in the title, first responsibility, or first requirement outweighs the same theme buried at item 9.
- **Repetition is the JD telling you what it fears.** The phrase that appears 3+ times across sections is the failure the last hire (or the absence of one) caused.
- **Verb strength ranks themes** — "own/drive/build" > "lead/manage" > "support/assist/collaborate."
- **What's missing is a signal too** — a theme a reader would expect for this role type that the JD barely touches usually reveals the operating model (no pipeline talk at a B2B company → probably not sales-led) or an org reality (another team owns it). Use the omission to sharpen the Emphasis read and translation — it is never its own output line (the `De-emphasized:` line was retired 2026-07-24, per the user).
- **The company decides the meaning.** Every ambiguous emphasized term (growth, demand gen, platform, partner) is resolved by research dimension 1 (GTM, tech, buyer) before it is mapped to the user's capabilities — never by the term's generic meaning.
